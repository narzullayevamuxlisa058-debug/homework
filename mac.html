It all began in 2006 when Apple transitioned to Intel processors, launching the very first MacBook Pro and a classic polycarbonate white-and-black MacBook that quickly became a massive hit among students and casual users worldwide. The defining moment in laptop history arrived in 2008 when Steve Jobs famously walked onto a stage and pulled the original, incredibly thin MacBook Air out of a standard manila office envelope. This legendary stunt introduced the world to the "Unibody" design—crafting a laptop chassis from a single, solid block of aluminum—which immediately set a new premium standard that competitors are still trying to match today. Over the next decade, Apple continued to push boundaries by introducing stunningly sharp Retina displays and eliminating bulky CD/DVD drives, though they also faced a controversial era marked by hyper-minimalist designs, unreliable "butterfly" keyboards, and the complete removal of traditional ports. However, the ultimate revolution occurred in late 2020 when Apple abandoned Intel to introduce its own custom-made Apple Silicon chips (the M-series).
